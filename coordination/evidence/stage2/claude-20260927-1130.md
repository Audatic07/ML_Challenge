# STAGE2-CASCADE: where the tune score goes, and a route toward 0.98

Session `claude-20260927-1130`, 27 September 2026, 11:30 to 14:45 IST. Branch
`codex/stage2-claude-20260927-1130`, based on `codex/v5-dist-claude-20260927` at `fdcfa30`.
Every number below comes from the fixed 100k tune partition of the v5.1 run or from the unlabelled
test scores of that run. The audit partition stays closed. I started no instances. The only spend
was about 5.9 GB of S3 transfer to this machine, roughly $0.65.

## Short version

The released package scores 0.9605 on tune. Its candidates are the top 30 per S1 by cosine
similarity, and their oracle is 0.9841. The same v5.1 model applied to the full retrieved union
(about 265 candidates per S1) reaches 0.9679 against an oracle of 0.9952. So most of the loss comes
after retrieval. True matches that were retrieved and then scored below the threshold cost about
1.9 points on their own.

Three measured changes bring tune to 0.9705. Ranking candidates by the model's own probability
keeps the full-union oracle with far fewer candidates: the top 12 with p >= 0.001 hold U at 0.9948
with 6.7 candidates per S1, about a quarter of the release. A second-stage model that compares each
candidate with the other strong candidates of its S1 adds 0.21 points in 2-fold cross-fitting.
Choosing each S1's number of matches by expected F0.5 adds 0.06 more.

About one point is still missing. The errors that remain are ones a pair model cannot settle,
because the answer depends on whether another S1 record owns the candidate. Competition features
are the most promising next step, and nothing here proves they close the gap. France is 15% of
test, has no labels, and shows signs of over-matching. It is the largest unmeasured risk.

## 1. Where the full-union score goes

At threshold 0.785 with the one-owner-per-target rule, v5.1 scores 0.96794 on tune (0.96777
without the rule). The oracle is 0.99520 and the total loss is 3.21 points. Each row below is a
separate counterfactual, so the rows do not sum to the total.

| Accept every retrieved true match now below the threshold | 19,800 | +0.0194 |
| Drop every false positive | 3,026 | +0.0079 |
| Retrieval loss (1 - U) | 4,619 unretrieved | 0.0048 |

17,177 S1 have at least one rejected true match, and 16,665 of them (97%) also have at least one
accepted match. A confident sibling therefore exists for nearly every rejected match. The rejected
ones sit behind their siblings in the ranking: REJECTED_RANKS. Of the 345,229 true pairs, 314,802
score above 0.9, and 6,013 fall between 0.785 and 0.9. The bands from 0.7 down to 0.05 hold 2,704,
2,632, 4,136, 3,487 and 3,448, and 3,388 score at most 0.05.

Every labelled target belongs to exactly one S1: 7,638,365 links point to 7,638,365 distinct
targets. 1,775 of the 3,026 false-positive targets (59%) belong to a different S1, and so do 81.8%
of all negative candidates. Tune scores 100k of the 2.2M train S1, so the one-owner rule almost
never fires there. It moved 5 true matches. On test every S1 competes for its targets (section 6).

## 2. Cut candidates with the model's own probability

The release ranks candidates for its top-30 cut by the sum of two cosines. The stage-1 probability
ranks them much better. With the threshold fixed at 0.785:

| Stage-1 cut (tune) | U | F0.5 | Candidates per S1 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| top 5 | 0.98794 | 0.96398 | 5.00 |
| top 8 | 0.99449 | 0.96792 | 8.00 |
| top 10 | 0.99481 | 0.96794 | 10.00 |
| top 10, p >= 0.001 | 0.99471 | 0.96794 | 6.38 |
| top 12, p >= 0.001 | 0.99481 | 0.96794 | 6.67 |
| top 15, p >= 0.001 | 0.99490 | 0.96794 | 6.94 |
| top 12, p >= 0.005 | 0.99445 | 0.96794 | 5.40 |
| top 30 | 0.99516 | 0.96794 | 30.00 |
| released cosine top 30 | 0.9841 | 0.9605 | 30.00 |

I would take the top 12 with p >= 0.001. It matches the top-10 oracle with a third fewer candidates.
On test the same floor with a cap of 10 leaves 7.0 candidates per S1: France 9.1, India 6.7, US 6.6.
France keeps more because its lookalikes score high (section 6).

This makes the design two-stage blocking. TF-IDF retrieval gathers about 256 candidates per S1, the
stage-1 LightGBM pair model keeps a short list, and the final matcher scores exactly that list.
`candidate_pairs.tsv` must hold the short list. The organizers review the code that produces the
candidate file, so the documentation should describe both stages plainly.

## 3. Stage-2 model with sibling features

`src/v6_stage2.py` builds 61 features for each survivor. They cover the stage-1 probability, its
logit, rank and gaps, S1-level summaries (best, second and third probability, sum, counts above 0.5
and 0.9), eleven S1-to-candidate text similarities recomputed with the v5 normalizer, lengths,
source, and sibling features. The siblings of a candidate are the five highest-probability other
survivors of its S1. Name, address, house-number and exact-match similarities to those siblings are
aggregated over confident siblings (p >= 0.5), weighted by sibling probability, and against the
single best sibling. All inputs are label-free: the S1 record, the survivors and their stage-1
scores.

Evaluation uses 2-fold cross-fitting by S1 hash. The tune S1 are out of sample for stage 1, so their
stage-1 scores are honest inputs. LightGBM ran with 63 leaves, learning rate 0.05 and early stopping
on an inner 10% of each training fold. CV_ITERS

CV_TABLES

Both folds and both countries gain. Stage 2 accepts CV_RECOVERED more true matches and CV_EXTRA_FP
more false positives. Single-match S1 get worse, CV_G1, because the lower threshold (CV_TH2 against
CV_TH1) admits extra candidates where one match is all there is. The model leans almost entirely on
stage 1. CV_GAIN

## 4. Expected-F selection per S1

For each S1, sort the survivors by probability and score every prefix by its expected F0.5, treating
candidates as independent and ignoring unretrieved positives. Predict the best prefix.

EF_TABLE

It helps S1 with one or two true matches and costs singletons EF_G0. On stage-1 probabilities the
same rule loses to a plain threshold (EF_P1), so it depends on the better calibration of stage 2.

## 5. What is left after stage 2

RES_SUMMARY

| Share of pairs with the property | Rejected true | False positives | Accepted true | All negatives |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
RES_TABLE

Two groups dominate, and a pair model cannot resolve either of them. Among survivors with a missing
address, RES_ADDR. The supplied data has many same-name records without an address, and most of
them belong to other S1. Among survivors whose name shares no token with the S1, RES_NAME. The
typical true case is a variant renamed to an invented word at the S1's exact address. The typical
false case looks identical and belongs to another S1 registered at that address. The deciding fact
is which S1 record the candidate resembles most, and nothing in stage 1 or stage 2 measures it.

## 6. Test checks without labels

TEST_COMP

TEST_SIZES

Inspecting French S1 records explains the gap. Names are built from a city plus a generic word
(school, association, committee, company), so several businesses at one street address share the
city token and look alike to a model trained on distinctive US and Indian names. It gives such
lookalikes probabilities near 1. The one-owner rule removes part of the damage, and competition
features would address it directly.

## 7. Route toward 0.98

| Step | Evidence | Cost | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Cut by stage-1 probability (top 12, p >= 0.001), then re-run the v5.1 threshold and final assembly | tune 0.9678 at top 10 against 0.9605 released, 6.7 against 30 candidates | about 1 h locally, no training | ready |
| 2. Stage-2 sibling model with expected-F selection | +0.27 points over step 1 on tune (0.9705) | about 2 h: test features (about 15 min), final model on all 100k tune S1, assembly, validation | code and tune evaluation done, test run not started |
| 3. Competition features from reverse retrieval (`src/v6_reverse.py`) | none yet | the India character search alone took 727 s on 20 local threads, so run it on the r-class SageMaker workers or only for uncertain pairs | written, partial run, no result |
| 4. France checks after every step | label-free distributions only | small | not started |
| 5. Recover retrieval misses (0.48 points on the union) | v5.1 benchmark | full feature rebuild, about 3 h | not started |

Steps 1 and 2 carry little risk and reach about 0.9705 on tune with 7 candidates per S1. The
remaining point most likely has to come from step 3, which is unproven. Open the locked audit once,
for the chosen champion only. Test F can move either way from tune F: the one-owner rule acts on
test in ways tune cannot reproduce, and France has no labels.
